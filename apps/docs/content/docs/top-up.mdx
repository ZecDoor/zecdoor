title: Top up and shield
description: How a balance below the bridge minimum moves anyway, in the same single transaction.
---

NEAR Intents only takes Solana ZEC deposits above a minimum. With ZecDoor's 0.25% fee, the smallest move it accepts is
about **0.00133669 ZEC**, and ZecDoor reads the minimum again before every move ([sources](/sources#oneclick)). On 6 October
2026, 17,647 Solana ZEC accounts were below the minimum but worth more than a move costs (about $0.63), and 73,312 more
held dust worth less than that ([sources](/sources#holders)). The top-up is for the first group; for dust, the app shows
that moving would cost more than the balance is worth.

Amounts on this page are also given in zatoshi, or "zat": 1 ZEC = 100,000,000 zat.

## What the one transaction does

1. **Swap.** Jupiter, Solana's main swap aggregator (its routing engine is called Metis), swaps just enough SOL or USDC
   into ZEC, as an *exact-output* swap: the output is exactly the missing amount, and the most you can pay is fixed by the
   quote, which allows at most 1% price movement ("slippage").
2. **Send.** All of your ZEC — what you had plus what the swap bought — goes to the NEAR Intents deposit address for your
   shielded address.

If any part fails, the whole transaction fails and nothing moves. Phantom shows both parts; that is expected. If the
transaction succeeds but the move then cannot complete, NEAR Intents refunds Solana ZEC, including what the swap bought,
to your wallet, not the SOL or USDC you spent.

On a minimum-size move the bridge's fee (up to 0.00032 ZEC) is about a quarter of the amount, and the deposit address's
token account costs about 0.0015 SOL ([fees](/fees)). Check the review screen before signing.

## A real example

A read-only simulation against mainnet on 5 October 2026, for a wallet holding 37,814 zat (0.00037814 ZEC):

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Missing to the minimum | 95,855 zat |
| Jupiter route | SOL → ZEC, exact output |
| SOL spent | 0.010558068 SOL |
| Sent to the deposit address | 133,669 zat |
| Transaction size | 1,030 bytes (limit 1,232; ZecDoor stays under 1,060 — [why](/sources#limits)) |
| Compute used | 147,488 units |

Simulations use `sigVerify: false` and send nothing. They are part of the test suite ([testing](/testing); all
results in [sources](/sources#simulations)).

## What the allowlist allows in a top-up

In plain words: the only extra thing the transaction may do is one swap whose output goes to your own ZEC account. In
detail, on top of the normal deposit, and nothing else:

- one Jupiter v6 `exact_out_route` or `shared_accounts_exact_out_route` (v1 or v2) whose **destination is your own ZEC
  account**, whose authority is you, whose input is your own SOL or USDC account, and which carries **no platform fee
  account**. The account layout is checked against the interface Jupiter publishes on chain (its IDL).
- wrapping your own SOL into your own wrapped-SOL account, capped at the swap's maximum input, and closing it again in the
  same transaction.
- creating your own ZEC account if you do not have one yet (you pay its rent; it stays yours).

The complete rules are in [Security](/security#the-instruction-allowlist).

## Where it is not offered

Jupiter's terms restrict the United States, China, Singapore, Côte d'Ivoire and Iraq. ZecDoor does not offer the top-up there.
Moves of balances already above the minimum still work. We have not confirmed whether this restriction applies to apps
that use Jupiter's API; ZecDoor applies it anyway ([availability](/availability)).
