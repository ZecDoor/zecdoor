## The route

```
Your Phantom wallet ──► one-time NEAR Intents deposit address (Solana)
                         └─► NEAR Intents pays native ZEC ──► your shielded address (Ironwood pool)
```

ZecDoor never holds the funds at any point. If a move cannot complete, NEAR Intents refunds the Solana wallet that sent
it, because every quote ZecDoor asks for names that wallet as the refund address.

## Step by step

<Steps>
<Step>
### You pick the amount and the destination

A wallet made in this browser (each move pays a new address of it), or an address you paste from Zodl, Zkool or another
shielded wallet. Pasted addresses are decoded in the browser: they must be unified addresses with an Orchard receiver,
because that is what NEAR Intents can pay ([sources](/sources#oneclick)). Sapling-only (`zs1…`), transparent (`t1…`),
TEX and testnet addresses are refused before any quote is asked for.
</Step>
<Step>
### The page checks the bridge and your region

It asks our server whether NEAR Intents reports an incident and whether ZecDoor is offered where you are
([availability](/availability)). It also downloads the OFAC list of sanctioned Solana addresses and checks your wallet
against it in the browser, so your address is not sent to us.
</Step>
<Step>
### NEAR Intents signs a quote

The browser asks the 1Click API directly for a quote: exactly your amount in, your Zcash address as recipient, your
Solana wallet as refund address, a 30-minute deadline. 1Click answers with a one-time deposit address and an ed25519
signature. The page verifies that signature and every field it covers, and checks the fields it does not cover (the fee
rows and the request deadline) against what it asked for. See [the proof](/proof#the-quote-signature).
</Step>
<Step>
### The page builds one transaction and checks it

The transaction may only contain what the [instruction allowlist](/security#the-instruction-allowlist) permits: send the
quoted amount from your own account to the quoted deposit address (and, for a top-up, one Jupiter swap into your own ZEC
account). Then it is simulated against current Solana state; the deposit address must receive exactly the quoted amount.
If any check fails, Phantom is never asked.
</Step>
<Step>
### You sign once in Phantom

One `signAndSendTransaction` call. There is no message signature and no token approval.
</Step>
<Step>
### The page follows the move

It reads the status from NEAR Intents every few seconds: deposit seen, converting, sent on Zcash. In 45 mainnet moves we
timed, this took about 2½ to 9 minutes, typically about 3 ([sources](/sources#timing)). You can close the page; the move
continues and the page picks up where it left off on this device.
</Step>
<Step>
### Your browser finds the note

For a wallet made here, the page downloads compact blocks from a public lightwalletd server and trial-decrypts them with
your viewing key, in a Web Worker. When it finds the note at your address with at least the quoted amount, it shows the
[proof](/proof).
</Step>
</Steps>

## What runs where

| Part | Runs in | Talks to |
|---|---|---|
| Quote, checks, transaction building, simulation | Your browser | NEAR Intents 1Click, Solana RPC, Jupiter (top-up only) |
| Seed, viewing key, addresses, arrival scan | Your browser (WebAssembly) | A lightwalletd gRPC-web server (zec.rocks) |
| Health, region, sanctions list, counter | Our server (a Cloudflare Worker) | NEAR Intents' status page and status API |

The complete list of services the app can reach is in its Content Security Policy, quoted in [Security](/security#content-security-policy).
